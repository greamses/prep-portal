Split

Cutting a block down

A split cuts a block across its longest side. When that side divides by the working base you get exactly base equal pieces — a cube falls into flats, a flat into rods, a rod into units. When it does not divide, the block falls into slices one unit thick. Double-tap a block on the mat to split it where it lies.

Merge

Trading back up

A merge is the trade going the other way. Pick blocks that are exactly alike and join them along their shortest side, so units make a rod, rods make a flat and flats make a cube. With trade rules on you may only merge exactly as many blocks as the base — ten in base ten, five in base five. Turn the rules off to join any number of matching blocks.

Bases

Changing the base

The base decides the grouping, and nothing else. In base five a rod is five units, a flat is five rods and a cube is five flats — and the bold lines on the paper mat regroup to match. The board keeps the running total in units and writes the same total in the base you are working in.

Sorting

Own sizes and highlights

Set a length, width and height of your own to build any cuboid — good for volume, factors and arrays. Highlight blocks in one of six colours to keep sets apart; the board totals each colour on its own, and Tidy lays the mat out in rows, one row per colour.
