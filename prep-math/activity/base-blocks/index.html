Blocks
Mathematics · Things to think with

Manipulatives.

An endless sheet of squared paper and a shelf of things to put on it. Build a number out of blocks and trade it up, slide the beads of a Russian, Chinese or Japanese abacus, or lay out a place-value chart and stand the blocks in their columns. Pick one to open the canvas.

Blocks

Splitting, trading, regrouping

A split cuts a block across its longest side — into base equal pieces when that side divides by the base, otherwise into slices one unit thick. Merging is the same trade backwards. Regroup does the whole job in one press: highlight anything at all and it rewrites those blocks the best way the base can write them.

Abacus

Three counting frames

On every frame the beads that count are the ones pushed against the bar. Tap a bead and it comes in, bringing the beads inside it; tap one that is already in and it goes out, taking the beads outside it with it. The schoty has ten beads to a wire with the middle pair darkened; the suanpan keeps two fives above the bar, the soroban only one.

Charts

Boards to work on

The place-value chart relabels its columns the moment you change the base, and reads back whatever blocks are standing in them. The multiplication and division grids are one table read two ways: tap a square and its row and column light up so you can see where the answer comes from, or blank squares out and try to fill them yourself.

Canvas

The paper has no edges

Everything shares one canvas, and it runs as far as you need — spread a thousand out if you like, then press Tidy to lay it all back in rows or Fit to bring it into shot. Switch between the 3D view and a straight-down 2D view at any time; the bold lines on the paper regroup with the base.
