Meet the Light Bulb

Look around your house in Lagos or Abuja. You will see a small glass bulb sitting on your ceiling. It has a rounded glass body and a metal bottom. Inside, there is a tiny wire that shines brightly when power comes on. It uses electricity to bring daylight into our rooms after sunset!

"

A light bulb turns invisible power into bright sunshine inside your house!

How It Glows Like Magic

Electricity moves through wires like danfo buses moving down a road. When you flip the switch, power rushes into the bulb. It squeezes through a tiny wire called a filament. The tiny wire gets super hot like a pot of boiling jollof. Because it is so hot, it begins to glow brightly! The clear glass keeps the air away so the wire does not burn up.

  • Filament - The thin wire inside that glows when hot.
  • Switch - The button you press to start electricity.
  • Glass Globe - The glass shell that protects the wire inside.
Why The Light Bulb Matters

Before light bulbs, night time was very dark and tricky. People used oil lamps that made dark smoke. Candles could easily fall and start bad fires. Today, light bulbs help doctors work in hospitals at night. Children can read books and play games long after the sun goes down.

  1. Step 1 - Flip the Switch.
    You press the wall button to clear the path for energy.
  2. Step 2 - Power Rushes In.
    Electricity zooms through the lamp wire into the bulb base.
  3. Step 3 - The Wire Glows.
    The tiny filament heats up quickly and lights your room!
