Bright Idea: Light Bulb

What if you could make night as bright as day? The light bulb turned dark rooms into playgrounds. Let’s see how a tiny filament can shine like the sun.

Prep Portal Science Desk· Science · History · Invention· Grade 4
Quick Facts
First year
Thomas Edison filed a patent in 1879 for a long‑lasting bulb.
Filament material
Early bulbs used carbon; modern ones use tungsten that glows at 2,700°C.
Power needed
A typical household bulb uses about 60 watts, like a small fan.
Meet the Light Bulb

The light bulb looks like a tiny glass ball with a metal base. You can see them in homes, schools and even on the streets of Lagos. Inside the glass is a thin wire called a filament that glows when electricity passes through it. The bulb keeps the glow safe from air, so it does not burn out quickly. It is unique because it changes invisible electricity into bright light.

"

The first bulbs lasted only a few minutes, but today they can shine for over 20,000 hours.

How it Works

When you turn on a switch, electricity travels like water through a pipe. It reaches the filament, a thin metal thread, and meets resistance – like a soccer ball hitting a wall. That resistance makes the filament heat up until it glows, just like the coals in a barbecue. The glass envelope stops air from touching the hot filament, so it does not melt. The light you see is the filament’s glow traveling through the bulb’s clear glass. This tiny glow can light up a whole room, similar to how a phone screen lights up when you press a button.

  • Filament - The thin metal wire that becomes hot and shines.
  • Glass envelope - The clear bulb that protects the filament from air.
  • Base - The metal part that screws into a socket to receive electricity.
Why it Matters

Light bulbs let us read, study and play after the sun sets, so schools can stay open later. They help doctors see patients at night in hospitals across Nigeria. By using less electricity than candles, they keep homes safer from fire. The invention also sparked new ideas like street lamps that guide danfo buses and markets at night.
