The Running Wonder: Basilisk Lizard

Did you know a lizard can sprint across water like a superhero? Meet the basilisk, the “Jesus lizard” that walks on rivers.

Prep Portal Science Desk· Reptiles·Adaptations·Science· Grade 3
Quick Facts
Water runner
It can dash across water for up to 15 seconds.
Length
Adults grow to about 60 cm long.
Speed
It moves at roughly 5 metres per second on water.
Meet the Animal

The basilisk is a small reptile. It has a long tail and a crest on its head. It lives in the rainforests of Central America, not in Nigeria. Its skin is dark with bright green spots. It can run on water, which makes it very special.

"

It can sprint across a river without sinking, just like a football player leaping over a puddle.

How it works

The basilisk slaps its feet against the water fast enough to create tiny air pockets. This is called hydrofoil action. The pockets keep the lizard from sinking, like a danfo bus skimming over a shallow creek. Its long tail acts as a rudder, helping it stay straight. The lizard also spreads its toes like a tiny paddle. All of this lets it zip across water like a phone sliding on a smooth screen.

  • Foot slaps - rapid hits push water down.
  • Tail balance - keeps the lizard steady.
  • Fast stride - each step is quick enough to hold air.
Why it matters

Running on water helps the basilisk escape hungry birds and snakes. It can chase insects that sit on floating leaves. The skill also lets it cross streams to find food. By staying dry, it keeps its skin healthy in the humid forest.

  1. Step 1 - Spot water.
    It looks for a calm river or pond.
  2. Step 2 - Sprint.
    It pushes off with its strong legs.
  3. Step 3 - Keep moving.
    It never stops until it reaches the far bank.
"

Besides water walking, the basilisk can also climb smooth rocks like a tiny ninja.
