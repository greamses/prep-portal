Quick Facts
Largest Brain
An orangutan’s brain weighs about 350 grams, similar to a small smartphone.
Long Arms
Their arms can stretch up to 2 meters, longer than a danfo bus aisle.
Longevity
Orangutans can live up to 60 years in the wild, like a grandparent.
Meet the Orangutan

Orangutans have shaggy orange‑red fur and long, strong arms. They live in the rainforests of Borneo and Sumatra, far from Nigeria. They are arboreal, spending most of their lives high in the trees, and they are famous for tool use. Their faces have big, curious eyes that help them spot ripe fruit from far away.

"

Orangutans can use sticks to fish for termites, just like a child uses a stick to poke a game controller.

How it works

Their brains have excellent memory, letting them remember where fruit trees grow for many seasons. They also show great problem solving, using leaves as umbrellas or building simple tools. Think of a phone that stores thousands of contacts – an orangutan stores hundreds of fruit locations. Like a football team planning a play, they plan steps before acting. This intelligence lets them adapt when forests change.

  • Tool Use - They pick up sticks or leaves to get food, showing planning.
  • Learning - Young orangutans watch mothers for weeks, like a tutorial video.
  • Social Play - They swing and play, strengthening muscles and brains.
Why it matters

Being smart helps orangutans find food when trees are far apart. Their memory lets them avoid dangerous predators and find safe routes. Tool use lets them get insects that other animals cannot reach, adding protein to their diet. All these abilities increase their chance to survive in a shrinking forest.

  1. Step 1 - Remember Food.
    They store the locations of fruit trees in their mind, returning year after year.
  2. Step 2 - Use Tools.
    When fruit is out of reach, they fashion sticks to pull it closer.
  3. Step 3 - Teach Young.
    Mothers demonstrate how to use tools, and the babies copy them.
