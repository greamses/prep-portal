The Indestructible Tardigrade: tardigrade

Did you know a creature smaller than a grain of rice can survive the vacuum of space? Tardigrades can live without water for years and still bounce back.

Meet the Water Bear

Tardigrades look like tiny eight‑legged bears with plump bodies. They live in moss, leaf litter, fresh water and even in the sand of the Sahara. In Nigeria they are hidden in garden soil and on the edges of rivers. Their secret is a special state called cryptobiosis that lets them pause life. They are also called extremophiles because they love extreme places.

"

A tardigrade can survive a trip to outer space and back!

How It Works

When water disappears, a tardigrade squeezes out almost all its body fluid and curls into a dry ball. In this anhydrobiosis it stops breathing, eating and moving – like a phone on airplane mode. Its DNA is protected by special proteins that act like tiny shields. The creature can stay in this state for years, then soak up water and wake up as if nothing happened. This superpower is why scientists compare it to a “biological backup drive”.

  • Cryptobiosis - a reversible pause of all life processes.
  • Desiccation - losing almost all water without dying.
  • Radiation resistance - surviving doses that would kill a human.
  • Extreme temperature tolerance - living in near‑absolute‑zero and boiling heat.
Why It Matters

The ability to shut down and restart helps tardigrades survive droughts, freezing nights, and even space travel. By studying them, scientists learn how to protect crops from harsh climates and how to keep medicines stable without refrigeration. Their proteins may inspire new ways to shield astronauts from radiation. In Nigeria, understanding these tiny survivors can help farmers keep soil life healthy during the dry season.

  1. Step 1 - Dry Out.
    The tardigrade loses water and curls into a tun.
  2. Step 2 - Pause.
    All metabolism stops, like a game saved on a cartridge.
  3. Step 3 - Reactivate.
    When water returns, it swells, uncurls and resumes life.

Try This

Tardigrade Rescue. Place a few dried beans on a plate, add a drop of water, and watch them swell back to life in minutes. Discuss how the beans are like tiny tardigrades waking from a dry sleep. Record the change in a notebook.
