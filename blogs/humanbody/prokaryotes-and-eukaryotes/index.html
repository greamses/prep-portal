Cell City Showdown: Prokaryotes vs Eukaryotes

Did you know some cells are so tiny you need a microscope to see them? What makes a prokaryote different from a eukaryote, and why does it matter for life in Nigeria?

Prep Portal Science Desk· Cells · Microbiology · Life· Grade 6
Quick Facts
Cell Size
Prokaryotes are usually 0.1‑5 µm, eukaryotes 10‑100 µm.
Nucleus
Only eukaryotes have a true nucleus.
DNA Shape
Prokaryotes have circular DNA; eukaryotes have linear chromosomes.
Meet the Cells

Prokaryotes are simple, single‑room houses that float in soil, water, and even the gut of a Nigerian farmer’s goat. Eukaryotes are bigger, multi‑room mansions with special rooms called organelles. Both live everywhere in Africa – from the Sahara sands to the rainforests of Cross River. Prokaryotes reproduce fast, while eukaryotes can build complex bodies like plants and animals. The key difference is the presence of a nucleus that stores the cell’s instructions.

"

One gram of soil can contain millions of prokaryotes, more than all the people in Lagos combined.

How they work

Think of a prokaryote as a speedy danfo bus – it can zip around, copy itself, and deliver nutrients quickly. A eukaryote is like a modern smartphone with many apps; each organelle does a special job, such as making energy or cleaning waste. The membrane acts like a fence, letting good things in and keeping bad things out. Inside, ribosomes are tiny factories that read the DNA recipe, just like a chef follows a jollof recipe. Together they keep the cell alive and ready for action.

  • DNA - carries the cell’s instruction manual.
  • Ribosome - builds proteins, the building blocks of life.
  • Mitochondria - power plants that turn food into energy.
Why it matters

Prokaryotes help decompose waste, turning kitchen scraps into soil for farms in Ogun State. Eukaryotes let us grow crops, raise livestock, and even heal when we get sick. Their different strengths keep ecosystems balanced, like a football team where each player has a unique role. Understanding both helps scientists create medicines and improve food production for Nigerian families.

  1. Step 1 - Prokaryote Power.
    These cells multiply fast, helping break down waste quickly.
  2. Step 2 - Eukaryote Advantage.
    They build complex tissues, allowing plants to grow tall and animals to move.
  3. Step 3 - Cooperation.
    Together they form healthy soils and clean water for communities.
